# Fueling Plan

Work out how many carbohydrates to take per hour on a long run or race, how big each portion is, and how often to take one.

::: warning General guidance, not medical advice
These numbers are starting points taken from published sports-nutrition guidance. Everyone's gut, effort and conditions differ. Practise your plan in training before race day, and talk to a clinician or sports dietitian if you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or take medication.
:::

---

## Where to find it

On the web, open the **Exercises** page and scroll to **Fueling plan**. Enter your duration, effort and gut training to see the plan.

In the mobile app, open **Library > Fueling plan**. Pick when to start, then tap **Set reminders** and the app sends a notification for each portion. Starting a new plan replaces the old one, and **Stop reminders** cancels it. Reminders need notifications turned on for the app.

Type the name of what you carry in **Fuel product** (it starts as "Energy gel"), or tap **Choose a saved food**. Each reminder has a **Log it** button: pressing it opens the app and adds one portion to today's diary under **Snacks** at the current time. With a saved food it logs one serving of that food; with a typed name it logs the portion as carbohydrates (4 kcal per gram). If the saved food has been deleted, the carbs are still logged under the name. Tapping the notification itself only opens the app. If the entry could not be saved, a message says so and you can add it yourself or press the button again.

Under **Fuel product**, type what you carry or choose a saved food. A saved food sets the portion size to its carbohydrates per serving, and the plan shows about how many you need in total. On the web, **Print schedule** opens a page with only your schedule (time, portion and carbs so far) so you can print it or carry it on race day.

## How the target is chosen

The default target depends on how long you will be running:

| Duration                         | Default target                      |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Under 60 minutes                 | No fueling suggested                |
| 60 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes | 30 to 60 g of carbohydrate per hour |
| Over 2 hours 30 minutes          | 60 to 90 g per hour                 |

Within the range, easy efforts (heart rate zone 1) aim at the low end and hard efforts (zone 5) at the high end. The default is also capped by how well your gut is trained:

| Gut training | Highest default |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| Beginner     | 45 g per hour   |
| Intermediate | 60 g per hour   |
| Advanced     | 90 g per hour   |

Higher intakes work best with a mix of glucose and fructose, and need practice. Start low and build up across your training block.

## Portion size and reminders

- The default portion is 25 g of carbohydrate, about one typical energy gel. Change it to match the product you carry.
- The time between portions is the portion size divided by your hourly target. For example, 25 g portions at 50 g per hour means one every 30 minutes.
- The first reminder comes within the first 45 minutes, then repeats at that interval until the planned finish.

## Your own numbers

You can replace the hourly target and the portion size with your own values. An override is used as typed, up to 120 g per hour and 100 g per portion. Your plan then shows the default range for the duration next to your number so you can see how far from the usual guidance it is.

## Race day and gut training

If you have a race, the plan can build up to your race-day amount gradually. Taking a lot of carbohydrate in a race that your stomach has never practised is the usual reason fueling goes wrong.

- The build-up starts at 30 g per hour and rises in even weekly steps, reaching your race amount two weeks before the race and holding it until race day.
- With less than three weeks to go there is not enough time to build up, and the plan says so. Stay at what your stomach already handles, or aim lower on race day.
- In the mobile app the race comes from your running goals (**Library > Training programs**), and the plan shows this week's practice amount with a button to plan your run at it. The weeks are counted from the day you first saw the build-up for that race.
- On the web, type the race day under the plan. The web does not remember a start day, so it shows the build-up as if you begin today.

## Drink reminders

Switch on **Drink reminders** in the mobile app to get a separate reminder to drink during the run. The default is 150 ml every 20 minutes (450 ml an hour); change both to suit you. How much you need depends on how much you sweat and on the weather, so these numbers are a starting point and not a target to replace everything you lose. A plan above 800 ml an hour is flagged, because drinking more than you lose can be harmful. The web shows the same drink plan and adds it to the printed schedule; it has no reminders.

## Apple Watch

Runs recorded on an Apple Watch can give fuel and drink reminders on their own, so they keep coming with your phone left at home.

- **Plan on the phone first (optional).** On **Library > Fueling plan**, tap **Use on Apple Watch**. The watch will offer that plan's timing and amounts when you start a run on it. Without this, it offers 25 g every 25 minutes and 150 ml every 20 minutes.
- **Turn it on for the run.** On the watch, open **Workout > Cardio > Alerts** and switch on **Fuel reminders** and/or **Drink reminders**. Change how often and how much with the plus and minus buttons or the Digital Crown. Like the pace and heart rate alerts, they are off until you turn them on and only last for that run.
- **During the run.** A strip under the clock counts down to the next reminder. When one is due the watch taps your wrist, the strip turns orange and says what to take (and says it aloud if **Voice cues** is on). Tap the strip to put it away. Pausing pauses the countdown. The gear button lets you switch the reminders on or off mid-run.
- **One or the other.** Use the watch's reminders or the phone's for a run, not both: the phone's reminders also show on your watch while the phone is locked, so you would be reminded twice.
- Reminders on the watch do not log anything to your diary yet, and Wear OS watches are not covered.
