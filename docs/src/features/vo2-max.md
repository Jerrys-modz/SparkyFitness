# VO2 Max

VO2 max is the most oxygen your body can use during hard exercise, in millilitres per kilogram of body weight per minute (**ml/kg/min**). A higher number usually means better aerobic fitness. SparkyFitness keeps your readings, shows how they change, and says how the latest compares with people of your age and sex.

## Where the numbers come from

There are two kinds, and the app never mixes them.

- **Readings** come from your watch or health source: Garmin, Polar, Fitbit, Oura, Google Health, Withings, Intervals.icu, ZeppBridge, and Apple Health or Health Connect through the mobile app (turn on VO2 max sync in the app's health settings). One reading per day is kept; if two sources report on the same day, the daily view averages them.
- **The run-based estimate** is worked out by SparkyFitness from your logged runs (pace, heart rate and climb) when you have at least two qualifying runs in the last six weeks. It is a model, not a measurement, so expect it to be off by several percent. It appears under **Reports → Exercise → Running Estimates** and, on mobile, stands in for the Dashboard trend only when you have no readings.

## Where to see it

- **Web:** **Reports → Exercise** has a VO2 max card under Running Estimates: the latest reading, a rating, a fitness age, the change over the report's dates and a line chart. The Diary's daily health card also shows the day's reading.
- **Mobile:** a **VO2 max** graph in the Dashboard's Health Trends (7, 30 or 90 days). Hide or move it in Dashboard Settings → Health Trends.
- **Compare:** **VO2 max** is one of the day-by-day metrics you can set against sleep, training load and others. It is not offered for "before and after" questions, because it drifts over weeks.
- **AI assistant and MCP:** ask "how is my VO2 max for my age?". The assistant reads your readings, how they moved, the rating and the fitness age, and keeps the run-based estimate apart and labelled as an estimate.

## Rating and fitness age

The rating (**Poor, Fair, Good, Excellent, Superior**) compares your latest reading with the Cooper Institute norms for your age and sex. It needs your **date of birth** and **sex** (male or female) in your profile, and covers ages **20 to 79**; without them the card tells you what is missing instead of guessing.

**Fitness age** is the age at which your reading would be typical for your sex. At the ends of the norms it reads "20 or younger" or "79 or older". It is a rough estimate from the same norms, not the fitness age your watch maker shows, and is not a medical assessment.

| Rating    | Meaning (percentile for your age and sex) |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Superior  | 95th and up                               |
| Excellent | 80th to 95th                              |
| Good      | 60th to 80th                              |
| Fair      | 40th to 60th                              |
| Poor      | below the 40th                            |

The norms are the Cooper Institute's VO2 max tables, as reprinted by Garmin ("VO2 Max. Standard Ratings", data reprinted with permission from The Cooper Institute).

## Good to know

- Readings change slowly. A move of about 1 ml/kg/min or less over a few weeks is steady.
- Different brands use different models, so compare readings from one source over time rather than across sources.
- A day with no new reading shows the latest earlier one on the web card, so the chart steps instead of breaking.
